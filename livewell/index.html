THE LIVEWELL REPORT

Longevity factors in Nigeria

Initial findings from a survey of 1,070 adults in Lagos, Kano and the FCT.

Scroll

1 in 8

young adults we surveyed, aged 18 to 35, already live with a long-term health condition such as diabetes, high blood pressure or heart disease.

Young people in Nigeria are facing a rising tide of chronic conditions, driven by economic pressure, changing lifestyles and limited access to care.

49%

Half of the households we surveyed manage a long-term condition, for themselves or a family member.

We looked closer at these households. Here is what we found.

POLITICAL AND SYSTEMIC STRESS COMPOUND THE DISEASE BURDEN

Families managing chronic illness are more likely to be let down by public services.

More than 1 in 3 households managing a long-term condition missed a public health service they needed.

Missed a vaccine, screening or medication in the past year.

8 in 10 households managing a long-term condition met corruption when using public services.

Experienced or witnessed corruption when using public services in the past year.

Differences are statistically significant: missed services p = 0.0087; corruption p = 0.0002.

ENVIRONMENTAL EXPOSURE INCREASES HEALTH VULNERABILITIES

Heat and polluted air are part of daily life, and harder on the sick.

Environmental hazards like extreme heat (69.7% vs. 65.4%) and air pollution (54.0% vs. 50.4%) show a higher baseline in NCD households. While environmental exposure is a population-wide baseline threat, for an NCD household (managing asthma, hypertension, or cardiovascular disease), those factors carry a far higher risk of medical complications and hospitalisation.

FINANCIAL DISTRESS IMPACTS HEALTH AND NUTRITION

52%

Half of households faced financial strain or went without treatment because of healthcare costs.

₦1,589

The minimum daily cost of a healthy diet for one adult, as of April 2026. Over a month, that is almost 70% of the national minimum wage.

When treatment costs this much, families cut back on nutritious food. Cheaper, less healthy diets then push up the risk of the same chronic conditions.

Source: National Bureau of Statistics (NBS), cost of a healthy diet, April 2026.

About this study

We ran a pilot survey with 1,070 adults across Lagos, Kano and the Federal Capital Territory (Abuja), asking about their everyday health, out-of-pocket costs and living conditions. We spoke with people in urban centres, peri-urban neighbourhoods and rural communities. Nigeria has one of the youngest populations in the world, and over two-thirds of our respondents were aged 18 to 35, split evenly between men and women.
