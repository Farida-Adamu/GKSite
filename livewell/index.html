ENVIRONMENTAL EXPOSURE INCREASES HEALTH VULNERABILITIES

Heat and polluted air are part of daily life, and harder on the sick.

Environmental hazards like extreme heat (69.7% vs. 65.4%) and air pollution (54.0% vs. 50.4%) show a higher baseline in NCD households. While environmental exposure is a population-wide baseline threat, for an NCD household (managing asthma, hypertension, or cardiovascular disease), those factors carry a far higher risk of medical complications and hospitalisation.

Methodology

We ran a pilot survey asking 1,070 people across Lagos, Kano, and the Federal Capital Territory (Abuja) about their everyday health, out-of-pocket expenses, and living conditions. To uncover the hidden, everyday friction keeping Nigerians from living longer and healthier lives. We spoke with adults living across urban centers, peri-urban neighborhoods, and rural communities. Because Nigeria boasts one of the youngest populations in the world, over two-thirds of our respondents were young adults aged 18 to 35, split evenly between men and women.

Our survey explored the daily trade-offs people make to stay healthy. We asked about chronic health conditions, catastrophic medical debt, environmental threats like extreme heat and air pollution, and where people go first when they get sick.

This release highlights initial findings.
